Reading guide & experiments to try

The generative story

For each topic k, draw a word distribution βk ~ Dir(η). For each document d, draw a topic mix θd ~ Dir(α); then for each position n, draw a topic zdn ~ Cat(θd) and a word wdn ~ Cat(βzdn). Only the words are observed (shaded node). The corpus here really was generated this way from three hidden themes (river, code, bakery), so we can check inference against the truth, which is never possible with real text.

Collapsed Gibbs sampling

θ and β are integrated out analytically (dashed nodes), leaving a Markov chain over the assignments z alone. Each step removes one token from the count tables and redraws its topic from the full conditional. The first factor asks “which topics does this document already use?” (the rich get richer), the second asks “which topics like this word?”. The trace shows log p(w, z | α, η); it fluctuates because the chain keeps sampling rather than optimising. Turn on collect samples after a burn-in to average the estimates across sweeps.

Mean-field variational inference

Replace the intractable posterior with a fully factored q(θ, z, β) = ∏ q(θd | γd) ∏ q(zdn | φdn) ∏ q(βk | λk), whose graph has no edges at all, and do coordinate ascent on the evidence lower bound (ELBO). Each update is deterministic and never decreases the ELBO. Note that the mean-field family must be initialised asymmetrically, otherwise every topic stays identical forever.

Experiments

  1. Set α = 0.05 and re-run: documents become nearly pure. Set α = 5: every document looks like an even mix, and the topics have to blur to explain it.
  2. Raise η to 3. The prior now insists every topic put mass on every word; watch the word distributions flatten.
  3. Reset twice with the same settings. The topics come back in a different order or colour: this is label switching, and it is why “compare with ground truth” has to match topics before scoring.
  4. Set K = 2 or 5 while the corpus has 3 themes. What does an unused or merged topic look like?
  5. Compare the two traces. They are different quantities (a log joint vs a lower bound on log evidence), so never compare their heights, only their behaviour: Gibbs wanders, VI climbs and stalls.
  6. Watch a single Gibbs step for a word like loop in a document that is mostly “river”: the document factor and the word factor pull in opposite directions.
